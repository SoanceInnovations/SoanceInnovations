# Scaling Mission-Critical Product Delivery for ProtoHarbor

Source: https://www.soance.com/case-studies/protoharbor-product-team.html

## Summary / TLDR

ProtoHarbor is a product design partner working on enterprise transformation programs. The team needed an embedded engineering team that could increase delivery capacity without weakening design quality or governance.

Soance shaped the strategy, experience, and technical foundation around measurable user and operational needs, then translated the concept into a launch-ready digital product.

## Overview

ProtoHarbor is a fictional product design company created for this representative portfolio story. Its operating context, user needs, and delivery constraints are modeled on realistic digital transformation engagements.

The core brief was to deliver an embedded engineering team that could increase delivery capacity without weakening design quality or governance while giving internal teams a system they could confidently operate, measure, and evolve after launch.

## Services provided

- Workflow discovery and service design

- Enterprise UX and design systems

- Application engineering and integration

- Quality assurance, documentation, and release support

## Challenges

The project needed to balance a polished customer experience with the less-visible realities of content, operations, permissions, integrations, and long-term maintainability.

- Cross-functional sprint teams: The experience needed to support this capability without adding unnecessary complexity for users or operational teams.

## Solution

We mapped roles, decisions, and exception paths, designed a shared component system, and delivered modular workflows that integrate cleanly with the wider technology environment.

- Designed and validated cross-functional sprint teams as part of one coherent, role-aware product journey.

- Designed and validated shared component and quality standards as part of one coherent, role-aware product journey.

- Designed and validated release planning and engineering documentation as part of one coherent, role-aware product journey.

A shared design system, instrumentation plan, and staged release approach kept the product coherent while allowing the team to learn from realistic usage patterns.
